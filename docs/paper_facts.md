### Notes on the claims

- **C1:** The brief's 'N -> nu mu mu' is right but less specific. The paper's neutrino is ν_µ and the pair is µ+µ− (Eq. (1) is typeset N → ν_µ µ^+ µ^−; the text layer flattens the sub- and superscripts). No W charge, mixing angle, Dirac/Majorana generator setting or Pythia process card is given. In the Fig. 1 display the red prompt track is labelled µ− and the black displaced pair µ− and µ+.
- **C2:** The substance matches. The PDF never writes 'ACTS fast simulation', 'ActsFatras' or 'Fatras'. 'Implemented in the ACTS framework' grammatically attaches to the detector geometry, and the fast simulation is cited to '[13] ... 2008 The Fast ATLAS Track Simulation (FATRAS) Tech. rep. CERN Geneva' (ATL-SOFT-PUB-2008-001). The paper gives no centre-of-mass energy, magnetic field, layer layout, material or Pythia tune.
- **C3:** Both numbers match exactly. The brief's label 'NN' is not the paper's notation: the paper writes 'denoted by heavy neutral lepton HNL, or simply N', and 'NN' occurs in the text layer only inside 'GNNs'.
- **C4:** The paper does not say what counts as a track for this number (no pT, η or hit threshold). A commented-out source line, not in the PDF, gives 'all: 138.4068' tracks per event. The Fig. 1 caption calls these 'underlying events'.
- **C5:** The paper mentions no separate validation sample and no per-stage split, and does not say how many extra training events were tried.
- **C6:** The training half of the claim matches word for word. The brief's C6 and C9 contradict each other in the same way the paper contradicts itself. The paper has no separate 'W' category: the W muon is the 'prompt track from the HNL process'.
- **C7:** The paper says 'true track' where the brief says 'particle'. The pT and |η| cuts are strict inequalities; the hit requirement is at least 5 spacepoints. There is no production-vertex, d0 or charge requirement, and the same definition applies to every category.
- **C8:** The criterion is double-majority matching, but the paper never uses that term. The Eq. (2) denominator has the typo 'reconstrutable', and the ϵ glyph cannot be extracted from the arXiv text layer. 'Track purity' appears only in the DBSCAN tuning sentence and is never defined. No fake or duplicate rate is defined or reported.
- **C9:** The numbers match exactly and are the same in the IOP version, which says 'tracking efficiency'. The paper does not say how the uncertainties were computed. Derived from the Fig. 3 vector data, not stated in the text: prompt = 4696/4744 = 0.98988 with binomial error ±0.0015, smaller than the quoted ±0.002; displaced ≈ 2639/2882 = 0.9157 ± 0.0052.
- **C10:** True edges connect 'two consecutive spacepoints coming from the same track'. The paper gives no breakdown by sub-step or by prompt/displaced, and does not say which sample (training or evaluation) the numbers come from.
- **C11:** 'thread' is a typo for threshold. The scores are the GNN outputs ('Edge score distribution from the Graph Neural Network', Fig. 2). The paper does not explain how edges, fake ones in particular, are assigned to the prompt or displaced category, or whether the 0.5 cut is applied before DBSCAN. Derived from the Fig. 2 vector data: prompt 138290/147349 true edges kept (0.9385) with 20657 fake (purity 0.8700); displaced 59848/64693 (0.9251) with 10753 fake (0.8477). These agree with the text to within 0.1 percentage point.
- **C12:** The paper names the parameter 'minPoints', not minPts. Ref. [2] is 'Farrar G R and Fayet P 1978 Phys. Lett. B 76 575–579', a mis-citation: in the LaTeX source the same key is the Introduction's long-lived-BSM citation. The PDF gives no layer sizes, kNN k or radius, filter cut, GNN hidden size or iterations, or the space DBSCAN runs in.
- **C13:** I searched the text layer for prompt, displac, large radius, radius, vertex, d0, impact parameter, mm, cut and threshold; none gives a numeric definition. The only length scales are 'Ø(1 mm)', a generic statement about LLP flight distance, and cτ = 100 mm. Large-radius tracks are said to 'have large impact parameters' with no value.
- **C14:** All four binning variables are truth quantities, but three are track parameters, not vertex coordinates, and there is no production-z panel. The radius panel spans 0-300 mm with axis label 'Production vertex radius [mm]'. The paper does not define the radius, and the 'Prompt' series spreads out to 200 mm in it (see Open points).
- **C15:** 'ttbar', 'tt̄' and 'top' do not occur. The only other dataset named is the TrackML challenge dataset, cited for earlier prompt-track results and not used for training here. Pre-training and transfer learning are not mentioned. The paper never says 'not ttbar' explicitly; that follows from the single sample it describes.
- **C16:** Each of 'file', 'releas', 'zenodo', 'download', 'public', 'sample' and 'seed' has 0 hits. 'availab' appears only in Ref. [13] and 'dataset' only for TrackML. ## Details useful for reproduction

### Physics sample and simulation

- **Hard process:** "The hard process is the heavy neutral lepton (HNL) production. pp → W → µN, N → νµ µ+ µ− , (1)" (p.3, Eq. (1)).
- **N mass and lifetime:** set by hand. "we set the N mass to be 15 GeV and its lifetime cτ = 100 mm" (p.1).
- **Model:** N is "a right-handed Majorana neutrino". No mixing angle, W charge, Dirac/Majorana generator switch or decay-channel settings are given.
- **Generation and simulation:** "generated by Pythia 8 [12] and simulated with the Fast Track Simulation [13] for a generic detector geometry implemented in the ACTS framework [14]" (p.3).
  - [12] is Sjöstrand et al. 2015, CPC 191 159 (arXiv:1410.3012).
  - [13] is the 2008 ATLAS FATRAS note, ATL-SOFT-PUB-2008-001.
  - [14] is Ai et al. 2021 (arXiv:2106.13593).
- **Pileup:** "No additional interactions are included, i.e., pileup events are not included." (p.3).
- **Event content:** "This process creates three muons." and "On average, there are 140 tracks per event, most of which come from underlying interactions." (p.3).
- **Samples:** "We generated 10000 events for training and 5000 events for evaluation. No improvement was observed when more training events are used." (p.3). Also: "All tracks are used to train the pipeline." (p.3).
- **Detector and run conditions:** not given. The detector is called only "a generic tracker detector" (p.1). The text has no centre-of-mass energy, magnetic field, layer radii, material, beam spot or Pythia tune; TeV, magnetic, field, barrel, beam, tune and seed each have 0 hits.

### Pipeline

- **Overview:** "The pipeline consists with three discrete stages, namely graph construction, edge classification, and track labeling." Details are deferred to Ref. [10], Ju et al. 2021, Eur. Phys. J. C 81 876 (arXiv:2103.06995).
- **Input:** "spacepoints are 3D position measurements from a tracking detector." (footnote 1). No other node features are mentioned.
- **Graph construction:** "The metric learning [9] is implemented as a MLP, projecting the 3D spacepoint position to an 8D metric space." A nearest-neighbour search in that space is followed by an MLP filter. Ref. [9] is Choma et al. 2020, arXiv:2007.00149.
  - Result: "keeps 99.9 % true edges with a purity of 5.7 %" (p.3).
  - Not given: kNN k or radius, filter cut, network sizes.
- **Edge classification:** "In this work, the Attention-based Graph Network originally developed in Ref [2] is used." It gives a score from 0 to 1 per edge.
  - Ref. [2] is mis-cited: "[2] Farrar G R and Fayet P 1978 Phys. Lett. B 76 575–579".
  - The working point is a 0.5 cut: "With a thread of 0.5 on edge scores" (thread = threshold).
- **Track labelling:** DBSCAN [11]. "minPoints are set to 2 and epsilon is tuned by trying values from 0.1 to 1 with a step of 0.1. Epsilon of 0.4 gives highest track efficiency and track purity." The PDF does not give the clustering space or metric.
- **Code:** "https://github.com/HSF-reco-and-software-triggers/Tracking-ML-Exa.TrkX" (footnote 2, for the GNN variants). No commit or configuration is given.
- **Training:** the only statement is "When training the pipeline, we monitor the edge efficiency and edge purity so as to determine how close the reconstructed graph is to the ideal graph." The paper gives no epochs, learning rate, batch size, optimizer, losses, layer sizes, seeds or validation set.

### Definitions and metrics

- **True edges:** "In an ideal graph, edges only connect two consecutive spacepoints coming from the same track, namely true edges."
- **Edge efficiency and purity:** "Edge efficiency is defined as the fraction of edges in ideal graphs that are kept in reconstructed graphs. Edge purity is defined as the fraction of edges in reconstructed graphs that connect two spacepoints coming from the same true track."
- **Reconstructable:** "A true track is considered as reconstructable when the track pT > 1.0 GeV and track |η| < 4.0 and it leaves at least five spacepoints in the detector."
- **Matching:** double majority, though the paper never uses the term. "greater than 50% of total spacepoints in the reconstructed and true track, respectively and simultaneously".
- **Efficiency:** ϵ = Ntracks(reconstructable, matched) / Ntracks(reconstrutable), Eq. (2) (sic).
- **Categories:** "prompt tracks from the HNL process" is the W muon and "displaced tracks from the HNL process" are the two N daughters. They are defined by origin, with no numeric cut.
- **Not defined or reported:** track purity, fake rate, duplicate rate.

## Open points (left unstated by the paper)

1. **Prompt vs displaced definition.** There is no numeric cut on d0, production radius or anything else. The categories are by truth origin: the W muon is "prompt" and the two N daughters are "displaced". The paper also does not say how fake edges in Fig. 2 and in the edge efficiency/purity numbers are assigned to the prompt or displaced category.
2. **Training data files.** The paper names no released files, dataset DOI or record, event ranges, seeds or data-availability statement. It says only that the authors generated 10000 training and 5000 evaluation events. It also does not say how many extra training events were tried, whether a validation sample existed, or how events were split per stage.
3. **Generator and detector configuration.** Not given:
   - centre-of-mass energy;
   - W charge (Fig. 1 shows a µ− prompt muon, while the unused source diagram has W+ → µ+);
   - HNL mixing, Dirac/Majorana setting and forced decay channels;
   - Pythia tune;
   - beam-spot or vertex smearing;
   - magnetic field, layer layout and material;
   - FATRAS/ACTS version and configuration.

   The only hint is a LaTeX source comment (not in the PDF) mentioning a TrackML geometry.
4. **What counts as a track** in "140 tracks per event": no pT, η or hit threshold is given.
5. **Network and training hyperparameters.** Not given: embedding and filter MLP sizes (only in source comments), kNN k or radius, the filter score cut, GNN hidden size, iterations and features, losses, optimizer, learning rate, batch size, epochs. The attention-GNN reference (Ref. [2]) is a mis-citation, and no code commit or configuration is given.
6. **DBSCAN details.** The PDF does not give the space or metric DBSCAN clusters in (a source comment says distance = 1 − edge score), whether the 0.5 edge-score cut is applied before clustering, or a minimum track-candidate size (a source comment says > 3, the PDF only minPoints = 2).
7. **Samples behind the reported numbers.** The paper does not say which sample (training or evaluation) gives the 99.9 % / 5.7 % graph-construction numbers and the Fig. 2 distributions.
8. **Uncertainties.** The method is not stated. The prompt ±0.002 is larger than the binomial error (±0.0015) of the 4744-track sample plotted in Fig. 3.
9. **'Production vertex radius' in Fig. 3.** The variable is not defined. Prompt (W-muon) tracks fill 0-200 mm with a half-normal shape (σ ≈ 55 mm), although they have |d0| < 50 mm and |z0| < 200 mm. This suggests the plotted radius includes z, for example a 3D distance, rather than being transverse; this is an inference.
10. **Track-level quality metrics.** Track purity (named only as a DBSCAN tuning target), fake rate and duplicate rate are not defined or reported.
11. **The all-tracks efficiency (0.9561 ± 0.0006)** is quoted even though Sec. 3 says only HNL-process muons are used for the efficiency. Its selection is presumably all reconstructable tracks, but this is not stated.
12. **Pileup:** none. Robustness to pileup and to other processes, and a comparison with conventional algorithms, are left to future work. The paper gives no timing numbers for its "no additional computing cost" claim.

## Identification of the paper's evaluation sample (our measurement, 2026-10-03)

The arXiv LaTeX source comments (not in the PDF) give per-event averages "hits: all: 1363.1512" and
"tracks: all: 138.4068". Over all 5,000 events of `WmuHNL15GeV100mm_nopileup_5K` our scan (derived/scans/v1) gives
exactly **1363.1512 hits** and **138.4068 particles with >= 1 hit** per event; the other samples give 1357.92 / 137.65
(10 GeV 100 mm), 1357.39 / 137.62 (15 GeV 200 mm) and 2026.52 / 199.91 (ttbar). So the paper's evaluation set is the
complete 5K file, corrupted event 4375 and event 4642 included (without our 2 dropped events: 1363.0164 / 138.3934).
Its training set (10k HNL events at 15 GeV / 100 mm) was not released.

## Reproducing the paper's denominators on the external set (our measurement, 2026-10-03)

Truth fits `derived/truth_fits/v1`, tables `derived/events/v1`; WmuHNL15GeV100mm_nopileup_5K without the corrupted
event 4375 (4,999 events). Paper numbers are from the Fig. 3 vector data (section "Values derived from the figures").

- **All tracks**: the paper's 0.9561 +- 0.0006 implies a binomial N of about 116,600 (99k-139k given the rounding of
  the error), 23.3 per event. Our targets (pT > 1 GeV, |eta| < 4, >= 5 hits, table values): 116,449 = 23.29 per event.
- **Prompt (W muon)**: the paper's 4,744 equals our count of W muons with >= 5 hits (4,744; the dropped event 4375
  has one more with 10 hits). With the pT and |eta| cuts on the table values we get 4,629: 113 W muons with
  |eta| = 4.07-4.32 and >= 5 forward hits, and 2 with table pT <= 1 GeV, drop out. So the paper's prompt
  denominator effectively applies only the >= 5 space-point requirement.
- **Displaced (HNL daughters)**: the paper's 2,882 (plus two bins drawn at efficiency 1) is about half of our
  5,623 daughter targets (5,866 daughters with >= 5 hits; 3,186 events have at least one; charges split 2,937 / 2,929).
  No simple half (one charge, leading or subleading pT, >= 3 pixel hits) reproduces the paper's |d0| spectrum, which
  is much harder than our truth-fit |d0| (paper: 37.6 % below 50 mm, 40 % above 100 mm; ours: 77 % below 50 mm,
  7 % above 100 mm). Instead the paper's "|d0|" distribution has the shape of our **transverse decay radius r_v** of
  the daughters, bin by bin (fractions in 50 mm bins 0.376 / 0.224 / 0.124 / 0.093 / 0.065 / 0.051 vs our r_v
  0.377 / 0.216 / 0.134 / 0.090 / 0.078 / 0.045), at a count ratio of 0.51-0.57 per bin. A true |d0| cannot exceed
  r_v, and for these boosted decays it is typically several times smaller (our median |d0| 17.6 mm, median r_v
  about 80 mm). Most likely the paper's displaced category holds one daughter per event and its "truth |d0|" behaves
  like the production radius. This is our inference from the figure data; the paper does not define either.
- Consequence for the comparison in brief §5f: prompt and all-track denominators can be reproduced (prompt with the
  >= 5-hit requirement only); the displaced one cannot, so the displaced comparison is made with our full daughter
  set and, as a check, with each charge separately.
